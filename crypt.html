And then you are murdered

The Crypt

You don't leave. You don't stop playing. And you are not finished being useful to your side.

What death costs you

Your allegiance becomes public. Murdered players are known Faithful. Banished players revealed themselves on the way out. Everyone in the room knows exactly what you were.

You still win or lose with your original team. A murdered Faithful only wins if the Faithful win. A banished Traitor still wins if any Traitor survives. You have every reason to keep caring.

◆

What the dead do

You play every challenge, in your own bracket. The dead compete against the dead, scored separately, running alongside the living.

And whatever you win, you cannot keep. Every token the dead win must be given to a living player.

  • You see what you've won. You choose who receives it.
  • The host announces who you chose, aloud, to the whole room.
  • The host never announces what you gave. Only you and the recipient know that.

So think hard. Everybody knows what you are. Everybody watches who you help.

A dead Faithful making a gift is publicly endorsing someone in front of the whole room. Get it wrong and you've armed a Traitor while the room watched.

A dead Traitor has the harder problem. You cannot help your side without pointing straight at them. Anything you hand a living Traitor is a spotlight. You may end up feeding the Faithful simply to stay invisible — or making a gift designed to bury someone innocent.

Every gift is evidence. Read them.

◆

Resurrection

Twice in the day, the dead may reach back into the ground and take one of their own out of it. Never before the third session, and never twice in a row — at least two sessions must pass between them.

  • Only the Faithful are eligible. Anyone murdered, and anyone banished who revealed themselves Faithful. Those banished as Traitors stay where they are, permanently.
  • The dead decide by majority vote. The host announces the result to the house.
  • The returner draws a sealed envelope and opens it alone. They come back with a new role — and it need not be the one they left with.
  • Nobody learns what they drew. Not the room. Not the dead who voted for them. Not the side they used to be on.
  • They play for the side they are now. Everyone else down here keeps playing for the team they started with.

Think about what this does to the rest of the day.

Up to this point, the graveyard has been the one honest thing in the house — the only place where you knew for certain what someone was. That ends the moment a chair is filled again. The person you all watched die as a Faithful may be sitting back down at that table working for the other side, and the only person in the room who knows is them.

And if you were the one this group voted out on a hunch, and you come back holding something new — well. That's between you and the rest of them.

The one unbreakable rule of the dead

Nothing you learned while alive leaves your mouth. No hints. No faces. No meaningful silence when a name comes up. No interesting eyebrows.

Your gifts are the only way you are allowed to speak. Break this and you sit outside until the reveal.
